### Reflections
#### A
In my parametric design, features DW and DL played a huge role. These cross sectional areas are what hold the vertical forces of the strap pulling down on the T-Bar. Although the numerical dimensions are the same as my calculations, my hand drawings were way off on scale. I questioned my parameters at first and had to double check myself because I questioned my own work.

#### B
I think it's more important to provide tighter tolerances on the vertical faces on the inside of the bracket to avoid the t bar from shifting from side to side if a load shifts. All internal faces are sliding fit and should only be allowed to move to a certain degree before forces are shifted to a side that is not made to handle those forces that surpass the safety factor. 
When features are held to tighter tolerances than necessary the price of production sky rockets. With tolerances beyond a couple of thousandths, you would need specialized machines, tools, climate controlled rooms, and qualified operators. Although precision is always preferred, it is not always needed.



### 2157 Reflections
#### A
Depending on the fit type, different tolerances are needed. A slide fit will need loose tolerances just as a press fit will need a tight tolerance and a running fit will need a snug but not tight tolerance.

#### B
Dimensioning and tolerancing is a universal language in manufacturing for how things fit together. This allows parts from different manufacturers to come together as one efficiently.
