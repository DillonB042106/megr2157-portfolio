
# Dillon Burnette
I am a junior year mechanical engineering student at the University of North Carolina at Charlotte. I plan to pursue a career with a focus on manufacturing and assembly line design. I strive to become an engineer that is able to streamline the manufacturing process while maintaining a reasonable deadline and cost. I am intrigued by the way different items are manufactured with design dictating how they are made. I enjoy the implementation of robotics and mechatronics in manufacturing. Although giving a large upfront cost, a robotic machine will provide more precise work with a smaller long term cost.

 I was drawn to mechanical engineering by my curiosity to see how things work and their true purpose. I have always been intrigued by cars and their complex systems that work together to provide transportation. I view a car as a body with a heart(engine), brain(ecu), and its own character. I love how a multitude of static, electronic, mechanical, and fast rotating parts create a perfect equilibrium to define some of the most used, complicated machinery we use today. All of these parts must function under immense stress while maintaining reliability and must be cost effective for most people. It is imperative that cars uphold and surpass modern safety standards. Due to these standards, over 12,000 lives are saved per year since 1970 since the implementation of the 3 point seat belt and structural roll bars.

I am becoming a better engineer daily by learning different problem solving techniques. I am learning how to approach problems with an open mind and explore multiple ways to solve problems. I find it best to work backwards from a problem in order to establish a general solution and then refining the process after. I intend on finding the best way to design and manufacture products with reliability, cost, adaptability, and sustainability to develop solutions.
